How we check every deal

DealHunt lists only deals a person actually read in a store listing or a named deals roundup. Nothing here is guessed, rounded from memory, or copied from another deal site's headline.

What we do for each deal

  • Read the price where it is sold. Each deal's sale price and usual price were read on 27 November 2026 from the store itself or from a deals source that names the store — RedmondPie, TradingView / Benzinga, Good Housekeeping, BuzzFeed, Neeshu or McGraw Media. The source and the check time are printed on every deal card.
  • Compute, never invent. The discount percent and the dollars saved are calculated from the two prices shown. A deal is listed only if the sale price is below the usual price.
  • Link to the store. Every card links to the product at the store that sells it, so you can confirm the price yourself before you buy.
  • Compare across stores. Where the same product is on sale at more than one store, the Compare prices page puts the prices side by side and marks the cheapest.

What the verdict means

  • NEW LOW — the lowest price the source recorded for this product, or 50% off or more. The evidence is printed on the card.
  • STRONG DEAL — 30–49% off a real usual price. A genuine cut, worth buying if you wanted the item anyway.
  • ROUTINE SALE — under 30% off. A real discount, but the kind of cut this product sees often. Wait if you are not in a hurry.

Sources

Every price on this site comes from one of these sources, read on 27 November 2026. The full list of deals, prices and check times is in the site's own data file.

    Prices move fast on Black Friday. A deal that was live when we checked it can sell out or change price within the hour. The store page linked from each card is the final word — if the price there differs from ours, trust the store.

    Cyber Monday

    Most of these sales run through Cyber Monday, 30 November 2026, but stock and prices are set by each store. Check the store link before you buy, and compare the same product at another store first — the cheapest store for one product is often not the cheapest for the next.
